# Swell and shelter sort the atoll reef

*A research report of 16 November 2026 for the reef's redesign, written by helpers from what they read on the web.
Nobody has checked its citations by hand. What we take from it, and what Ryan decided, is in [`../REEF.md`](../REEF.md).
"The designer" below is Ryan; "the game's models" are the coral models as they stood that day.*

The designer's hunch runs mostly the wrong way. Of the seven coral and clam models the game already has, five belong in the top few metres, not on the drop-off. Cauliflower Pocillopora and bushy Acropora are the stout, wave-proof corals of the crest and upper fore reef. Giant clams crowd shallow reef tops and lagoon margins, because the algae they live on need light. The flat-topped head with rubble at its foot is the shape of a microatoll, a storm-thrown block or a lagoon knoll, and all three are shallow. Lobed Porites and staghorn belong to the lagoon and the lee. Only table coral, and Porites in a flattened plating form, belong on the 20-30 m drop-off. On real atolls that is a thinly covered slope of plates, lettuce-like sheets, pink coralline crust and Halimeda, with soft corals and sea fans where the current runs. The game's distances fit a measured atoll closely: its 145 m from beach to crest edge matches the 150 ± 60 m of ocean-side reef flat measured in front of French Polynesian islets. That means the 1954 US Geological Survey (USGS) survey of Bikini can supply real sizes for nearly every structure. Four sourced causes make the reef differ along the shore at the same distance out:

- the angle to the swell;
- whether the reef bulges towards the sea or curves away from it, which turns the crest from a 0.6-1.1 m step cut by blowholes into a 0.15-0.3 m arch with rich coral behind;
- storm history;
- nearness to the pass.

Every region can be tied to a documented food or material. Two examples: the spiny lobster that hides in the spurs by day and walks onto the flat at night, and the bonefish that run through shallow channels after full moon. The common injuries are minor: coral cuts, urchin spines, fire coral. The deadly ones are rare and tied to place and behaviour: stonefish and cone snails on the flat, the current in the pass, blackout on the drop-off. By number of cases, the leading danger is eating the wrong fish. Ciguatera rates in Tokelau, French Polynesia, Tuvalu and Kiribati are the highest reported among Pacific island groups. A central Pacific atoll has no box jellyfish, no crocodiles and almost no sea kraits, and the game has to choose a longitude: true giant clams, bear-paw clams and seagrass stop in Micronesia and western Polynesia.

**How to read this report.** A sentence that carries a link rests on that source. *(inference)* marks reasoning from the sources applied to this atoll. *(unsourced)* marks general knowledge that this research did not check. Some sources are weak: search-engine summaries, dive operators' pages and Wikipedia pages with missing citations are flagged where it matters. Most measured sizes come from one excellent source, the USGS survey of Bikini and nearby atolls in the Marshall Islands, made in 1946-1950, when the atoll was still unfished ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). Bikini is a large atoll. Its reef-flat and crest structures are set by the waves, not by the atoll's size, so they carry over to a small island; its lagoon-scale figures do not *(inference)*. Food and fishing come chiefly from a full study of Atafu in Tokelau ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)) and from species database pages. SeaLifeBase (SLB) and FishBase (FB) pages are cited as "SLB: species" or "FB: species", and their URLs follow one pattern. Distances in this report are measured from the ocean beach, as in the game's brief.

## Five of the seven models belong in the top ten metres

The rule that sorts the corals is exposure, then depth. On wave-exposed Tuamotu fore reefs, dense branching coral runs from near shore to 5-10 m: "stout, bushy, tree-like Acropora, branching Pocillopora, finger-like Porites". This gives way to larger boulder and plating corals at 10-20 m, and then to plating Porites and Pachyseris ("overlapping sheets, lettuce corals") at 20-30 m. Leeward reefs instead carry Porites, plating Montipora, small Favia boulders and columns of Pavona ([Living Oceans: Rangiroa, Aratika, Raraka](https://www.livingoceansfoundation.org/reefs-around-rangiroa-aratika-and-raraka/)). Giant clams are densest on shallow reef flats and edges under 10 m, and denser in enclosed lagoons than on outer slopes, because light limits how deep they can live ([Frontiers in Marine Science 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2020.592852/pdf)). At the deep end, a leeward Bikini wall has coral on "somewhat less than 10 percent" of its surface ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)).

The drop-off therefore needs models the game lacks:

- plating and sheet corals: Pachyseris "lettuce", plating Porites and Montipora;
- Pavona columns;
- lumpy, algae-crusted masses: on the leeward Bikini cliff, "rounded masses that may be old coral heads overgrown with algae", with green Halimeda and pale patches on small ledges ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)).

Where current runs, add the following. Tree-like soft corals (Dendronephthya) grow at 3-45 m in shade ([Reef Guide: Dendronephthya](https://reefguide.org/indopac/dendronephthyaspp.html)). Black coral would be rare above 30 m, because nearly 75% of its species live only below 50 m ([Wikipedia: Antipatharia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipatharia)). Sea fans belong on pass walls (dive-operator source, below).

The shallows lack four things:

- **Fire coral (Millepora):** upright yellow-green or light-brown blades, in colonies often 2-3 m across ([Wikipedia: Millepora platyphylla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millepora_platyphylla)).
- **The pink coralline ridge**, with its blowhole mounds.
- **Blue coral (Heliopora) heads**, if the atoll is Marshallese (its range was not checked).
- **Soft-coral and zoanthid carpets** for the lee.

## The ocean side follows one measured profile from beach to drop-off

The game's proportions are realistic. Remote sensing of 795 French Polynesian islets puts the ocean reef flat in front of islets longer than 1 km at **150 ± 60 m** ([Frontiers in Earth Science 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1123339/full)). A measured Bikini traverse runs **216 m** from reef edge to vegetation, made up of:

- a buttress and surge-channel zone, 15 m;
- algal pavement, 11 m;
- a coral-algal zone, 9 m;
- a reef-patch zone, 49 m;
- reef flat, 69 m;
- a hummock zone, 8 m;
- beachrock, 35 m;
- beach, 21 m.

Measured ridge widths of 26-35 m bracket the game's 27 m crest ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). Two differences matter.

**The real windward flat is shallower than the game's.** Atoll reef flats are usually under 3 m deep ([Frontiers search summary](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2019.00245/epub)). Bikini's storm-side flats lie under only 0.3-0.9 m at low tide ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)).

**A real algal ridge stands at the waterline, not 0.65 m below it.** Its top is set by how high waves wash at low tide, and it rises 0.6-1.1 m above the flat behind it (same source). Atafu's name for the reef edge, *fagautua*, means the part "partially higher than sea level at low tide" ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)).

Both are fair liberties for a world with a still sea level. If tides are ever added, the crest should break the surface at low springs and much of the flat should drain *(inference)*.

### 1. Ocean beach and sandy shallows: beachrock, foram sand and shark nurseries

**Ground and structures.** On a measured Bikini islet the beach is fronted by a belt of **beachrock 11-35 m wide dipping 7-9° seaward**. Beachrock makes up about 15% of the beach area there and is almost all intertidal. It is laminated in layers of 1.6-25 mm, polished and potholed on top where sand scours it, and "extremely rough, pitted and cuspate", grey to dark brown, lower down, with pits up to 0.9 m across ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). It cements quickly. A Japanese glass fishing float was found set into Bikini beachrock, and on Enewetak shell fragments, a bottle and a brass cartridge case were cemented in within about six years (same source). That is a sourced excuse for flotsam embedded in the game's beach rocks. Beachrock generally splits into blocks lying in several shore-parallel bands ([Wikipedia: Beachrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beachrock)).

Next to the beach the real flat "rises above low-water level" into hummocks of firm sand with shallow pools of living Porites, about half of it dry at low tide. Its rock is veneered with a 2-5 mm mat of living foraminifera (single-celled shelled organisms) bound by algae ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). The sand is largely foraminifera too (Amphistegina, Baculogypsina, Calcarina, Marginopora) on Funafuti ([Funafuti sediment record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi...........382cf8fa4dddb23913e95912f9386cec)).

Many islets sit on a cemented "shingle conglomerate" of rounded coral heads. It stands 0.3-1.2 m above the flat, ends in a ledge 0.3-0.6 m high, and sends rock "groins" out onto the reef. Only on Bikini's storm-exposed south side is the beach backed by **boulder ramparts** of blackened coral heads. They crest 1.5-1.8 m above high tide, are up to 30 m wide, are often doubled, and have worn giant-clam shells among the boulders ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). The game's south shore faces the swell, so it is the side for ramparts. Its 1 m deep shallows work if dressed as foram sand with beachrock ledges, pools and small Porites heads *(inference)*.

**Growth.** Little grows here. Wells's Marshall Islands sequence starts with a Porites lutea zone nearest shore ([Marshall survey citing Wells 1954](https://rminucleardocs.icaad.ngo/en/entity/bqu6gixsctv?page=141)). Near the Bikini beach the microatolls are smaller and more scattered, Porites lutea replaces blue coral, and nodules of coralline algae 2.5-5 cm across are abundant ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). A reef flat in Papua New Guinea, a high-island comparison, carried brown Sargassum, Padina and Dictyosphaeria on its inner band ([macroalgae zonation](https://core.ac.uk/download/39833364.pdf)).

**Animals and reasons to visit.** The shallows are a shark nursery.

- **Shark pups.** Blacktip reef shark pups of 35-61 cm use very shallow sand flats at Palmyra ([Papastamatiou et al. 2009](https://labs.eemb.ucsb.edu/caselle/jennifer/sites/labs.eemb.ucsb.edu.caselle.jennifer/files/pubs/papastamatiou_et_al_jfb_2009.pdf)). They are born in September-November and gather over ground flooded at high tide ([Wikipedia: Blacktip reef shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacktip_reef_shark)). At Kwajalein, small blacktips come right in to the beach after prey on the rising tide ([Underwater Kwajalein](https://www.underwaterkwaj.com/uw-misc/shark/Carcharhinus-melanopterus.htm)).
- **Fish.** Goatfish forage in "shallow sandy areas" ([FB: M. flavolineatus](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Mulloidichthys-flavolineatus.html)), juvenile emperors keep to shallow sand ([FB: L. olivaceus](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Lethrinus-olivaceus.html)), fringelip mullet school in pools and on reef flats ([FB: C. crenilabis](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Crenimugil-crenilabis.html)), and young convict surgeonfish are abundant in tide pools ([FB: A. triostegus](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Acanthurus-triostegus.html)).
- **Shellfish and crabs.** Spider conchs lie shallowly buried in sand and rubble ([SLB: Lambis lambis](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Lambis-lambis.html)). Nerites stay buried by day and graze rock at night ([SLB: Nerita polita](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Nerita-polita.html)). The chiton Acanthopleura gemmata lives on rock at the high-tide mark ([SLB: A. gemmata](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Acanthopleura-gemmata.html)); putting it on beachrock is *(inference)*. Grapsus rock crabs live in the splash zone, and on Atafu they are netted for food or bait ([SLB: Grapsus](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Grapsus-tenuicrustatus.html); [Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)).
- **Above the waterline.** Green turtles nest from October to April, peaking in January-February, at Tetiaroa ([Te Mana o Te Moana](https://www.temanaotemoana.org/en/the-green-sea-turtle-nesting-season-is-coming-to-an-end/)), and Napuka islanders "avidly sought" the eggs ([ANU Press, Napuka](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p337293/html/ch05.xhtml)). Coconut crabs of up to 4.1 kg are taken at night in the groves ([Wikipedia: Birgus latro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birgus_latro)).
- **Drift.** The windward beach collects it. Marshall Islands beaches have received pumice (some attributed to Krakatau), a pumice lump carrying usable obsidian, drifted canoes, Californian fir logs and a Costa Rican seed carried at least 11,000 km ([SPREP scan](https://library.sprep.org/sites/default/files/28_7.pdf), search summary).
- **Flying fish at night.** The shallows are the launch for torching. Atafu fishers take flying fish "in waters close to the shore" with coconut-leaf torches and scoop nets, up to 300 a night, mostly from July to October, and avoid the bright full-moon nights ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)).
- **Tool stock.** Worn clam shells in the rampart can be used, because western Pacific beaked adzes were made almost entirely from Tridacna hinges ([Craib 2023](https://pacificarchaeology.org/index.php/journal/article/download/54/37/234), search summary); linking the two is *(inference)*.

**Dangers.**

- **Stingrays** bury in sandy shallows and are stepped on. The pain is worst in the first 30-60 minutes and lasts up to 48 hours, and sliding the feet makes them leave ([Wikipedia: Stingray injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stingray_injury)).
- **Blacktip sharks.** Most blacktip attacks are bites to waders' legs and feet, usually without serious injury. Marshallese practice is to submerge the body to discourage them ([Wikipedia: Blacktip reef shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacktip_reef_shark)).
- **Stonefish** sting people wading on reef flats or walking beaches at low tide ([stonefish fact sheet](https://hollowaysbeacheec.eq.edu.au/SupportAndResources/FormsAndDocuments/Documents/fish-stone%20fish.pdf), search summary).
- **Sun.** At a UV index of 12 skin burns in about 12 minutes ([NIWA](https://niwa.co.nz/atmosphere/uv-and-ozone/uv-index-information)).
- **Currents.** Waves breaking on the crest pile water onto the flat. Where the setup is uneven it drives currents along the flat that return to sea through gaps ([reef-flat hydrodynamics review](https://www.sciopen.com/article_pdf/10.26599/HYD.2025.9380007.V2.pdf), search summary). The set along this beach should therefore run towards the inlet and the pass *(inference)*.
- **Beachrock** cuts *(unsourced)*.

**Along the shore.** Beachrock covers only part of a beach (15% at Bikini), ramparts only the storm side, and conglomerate groins only where islets sit on old platform ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). Drift collects on the windward beach ([SPREP scan](https://library.sprep.org/sites/default/files/28_7.pdf)). A walk along the south beach can therefore pass open foram sand with shark pups, beachrock slabs with chitons and embedded flotsam, a black coral-head rampart, and a conglomerate groin running into the shallows *(inference)*.

### 2. The gardens: microatolls, pools and storm blocks in the richest shallow coral

**Ground and structures.** Bikini's storm-side south flats are a close match for the game's south gardens: narrow (61-152 m), under 0.3-0.9 m of water at low tide, and with coral covering **half to three-quarters** of the bottom ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). Measured windward traverses put a coral-algal zone of 9-120 m (40-75% coral) and a reef-patch zone behind the ridge. Shoreward of those comes a nearly bare rock flat of 69-203 m with 5-10% coral in colonies under 0.3 m.

The nearest real thing to a "moat" is the outer blue-coral zone. It is "distinctly lower" than the zones to seaward, lies under 0.3-1.2 m at low tide, and has heads 0.9-7.6 m across rising from its floor: blue coral (Heliopora) and Acropora palifera microatolls, many tips breaking the surface at low tide. Blue-coral colonies 6-15 m across grow in 1.2-1.5 m of water (same source). Microatolls grow sideways once exposure to air stops them rising, and reach about 6 m across ([Wikipedia: Microatoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microatoll)).

Tide pools on a measured traverse:

- **Pothole pools:** two pools 2.7 and 1.5 m across and 0.9-1.5 m deep. Their walls are steep to overhanging "like large potholes", their rims are encrusted with fire coral and Acropora, and their sand floors hold cobbles of up to 15 cm.
- **Acropora pools:** others, up to 1.5 m across, are carpeted by a single brown Acropora colony.
- **Surge-channel pools:** along roofed surge channels, open pools 0.6-1.8 m across appear every 15-30 m.

Source: [USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf).

**Storm-thrown blocks** are usually **1.5-3 m across and 0.9-1.8 m high**. The largest is 10 × 7.3 × 3.7 m, about 200 tons. They lie in a narrow band 30-90 m behind the reef edge, which in the game falls 55-115 m out, inside this region. Where abundant, their silhouette is "a picket fence". The corals in them are tilted or upside down. Their bases are notched by solution into mushroom shapes, deepest at high-tide level. Above high tide they are blackened and jagged with spines; just below it they have smooth rounded pits (same source). Tuamotu reef-top boulders were torn from the outer slope at 5-20 m ([Coral Reefs 2018](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/10.1007/s00338-018-1732-8)). No measured reef-flat pool reaches the game's 4 m; the deepest flat hollows found are roofed caverns 2.4-3 m deep, so 4 m pools are a liberty *(inference)*.

**Growth.** Behind the ridge, coral is "encrusting, low, and massive or with short, stubby branches", with pink pavement and nodules of coralline algae in between. North of Enyu, brownish Pocillopora are numerous and purple algae cover about 30% ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). Wells's sequence from shore to ridge gives a ready gradient across the gardens: Porites lutea, then blue coral, then Acropora palifera, A. digitifera, and finally A. cuneata on or just behind the ridge ([Marshall survey citing Wells](https://rminucleardocs.icaad.ngo/en/entity/bqu6gixsctv?page=141)). Porites heads that grow up to sea level die on top and are colonised by branching Acropora and Pocillopora ([Living Oceans](https://www.livingoceansfoundation.org/reefs-around-rangiroa-aratika-and-raraka/)). Sea grapes grow long upright branches on calm reef flats and short, sturdy ones on wave-exposed edges ([SLB: Caulerpa racemosa](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Caulerpa-racemosa.html), search summary). Sheltered back reefs have higher coral cover but lower diversity than the fore reef ([Williams et al. 2013](https://peerj.com/articles/81)).

**Animals and reasons to visit.** This is the atoll's larder.

- **Nursery.** Back reefs and patch reefs are nursery habitat for reef fish ([atoll fish assemblages](https://www.alr-journal.org/10.1051/alr:2006021)), and juvenile humphead wrasse live in dense branching coral ([Wikipedia: Humphead wrasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphead_wrasse)).
- **Octopus.** Day octopus dens in crevices, under overhangs and in holes dug in rubble, and gives itself away by a **midden of shells** outside ([Wikipedia: Octopus cyanea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octopus_cyanea)). Atafu took octopus with a Pemphis stick and a coconut-frond lure, with a cowrie-and-pandanus lure, or (now mostly) with a metal stick at low tide. It is the main bait for lagoon line fishing ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)).
- **Giant clams.** Tridacna maxima is the commonest giant clam in the Marshalls ("hundreds in a short time" on some reefs). It bores into reef tops and must be pried out ([Kwajalein clams](https://www.underwaterkwaj.com/shell/tridacna/tridacnidae.htm)). On Napuka it is the staple of the December-March swell season, and certain coral clusters were reserved for feasts ([ANU Press, Napuka](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p337293/html/ch05.xhtml)).
- **Other low-tide food.** Spider conchs are taken by hand at low tide ([SLB: L. truncata](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Lambis-truncata.html)). Collector urchins live on rubble and among rocks ([SLB: Tripneustes gratilla](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Tripneustes-gratilla.html)), and Fijian women open them beside pools at low tide for the roe ([Nalasi, SPC Women in Fisheries 39](https://www.spc.int/DigitalLibrary/Doc/FAME/InfoBull/WIF/39/WIF39_08_Nalasi.pdf), search summary). Lollyfish (87% of all sea cucumbers counted at Pohnpei) and greenfish are picked up by hand at low tide ([SLB: Holothuria atra](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Holothuria-atra.html); [SLB: Stichopus chloronotus](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Stichopus-chloronotus.html)). The honeycomb grouper Epinephelus merra and squirrelfish shelter under the mounds, and Atafu took them with small long-handled scoop nets ([FB: E. merra](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Epinephelus-merra.html); [Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)).
- **Lobster at night.** The pronghorn spiny lobster leaves its crevices for the surge channels and walks up onto the flat. Atafu caught it with "feet and hands... during a rising tide on a moonlit night" ([Kwajalein lobster](https://underwaterkwaj.com/uw-misc/lobster/Panulirus-penicillatus.htm); [Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)).
- **Tool shell.** Tridacna, helmet shells and conchs were the adze stock of the Marshalls ([Habele Institute](https://habeleinstitute.org/w/images/5/5b/8I8F29LW.pdf), search summary).
- **Sights:** blue microatoll fields, pothole pools and the black picket fence *(inference)*.

**Dangers.**

- **Stonefish** lie half-sunk in sand and rubble and may be covered by only a few centimetres at low tide ([stonefish fact sheet](https://hollowaysbeacheec.eq.edu.au/SupportAndResources/FormsAndDocuments/Documents/fish-stone%20fish.pdf), search summary). Their spines can pierce boot soles ([Wikipedia: Synanceia verrucosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synanceia_verrucosa)).
- **Long-spined urchins** (Diadema savignyi, Echinothrix) hide in crevices and under boulders by day, often clustered, and spread out to graze at dusk ([Wikipedia: D. savignyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diadema_savignyi); [Wikipedia: Echinothrix diadema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echinothrix_diadema)). Their hollow spines break off in the skin ([DAN: Sea urchins](https://dan.org/health-medicine/health-resources/diseases-conditions/sea-urchins/)).
- **The geographer cone** lives on sand and rubble and under rocks, from the intertidal to about 20 m. It accounts for about 57% of known human cone stings and probably every death. The review counts 36 deaths among 141 recorded injuries from 1670 to 2017, though its own table is inconsistent ([PMC6356772](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6356772/)). Picking up a live shell is the trap *(inference)*.
- **Toxic crabs.** The brightly patched xanthid crab Zosimus aeneus of intertidal flats carries tetrodotoxin and saxitoxin, and half of Philippine poisonings from it were fatal ([Wikipedia: Zosimus aeneus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimus_aeneus)).
- **Morays.** Moray bites are rare and fall "mainly [on] fishermen while manipulating hooked or netted fish" ([Barreiros & Haddad 2008](https://scielo.br/j/jvatitd/a/Ct7YgbjGh6DrpV4wV3GL8NR/?lang=en)). A hand put blind into an octopus hole is the obvious way to meet one here *(inference)*.
- **Coral cuts** invite Vibrio infection ([DAN: coral cuts](https://dan.org/?p=3553)). One snorkeler spent ten days in hospital after rinsing a cut only in seawater ([MedAire case](https://www.medaire.com/case-studies/coral-injury-leads-to-severe-infection)).

**Along the shore.** Where the reef curves away from the sea, a low ridge has "a rich coral flat behind it". Where it bulges seaward, off islets, a high ridge leaves "few corals" behind. Storm blocks are commonest on certain reaches and rare along long windward stretches ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). At the same 80 m out, one stretch of the south gardens can be a dense coral garden with brown Acropora pools, the next a bare pavement dotted with microatolls, the next a fence of black blocks *(inference)*.

### 3. The reef crest: a pink algal ridge cut by surge channels and blowholes

**Ground and structures.** The crest is built by **crustose coralline algae** (a rock-hard pink alga), not coral. Algal ridges are common on open-ocean trade-wind reefs in the Indo-Pacific and absent from sheltered ones ([Atoll Research Bulletin, search summary](https://dn720400.ca.archive.org/0/items/atollresearchbu435449smit/atollresearchbu435449smit_hocr.html)). Bikini's ridge comes in two types:

- Off islets, on reef that bulges seaward, it is "a conspicuous feature rising 2 to 3½ feet" (**0.6-1.1 m**) above the flat, cut through by surge channels.
- Between islets, on reef that curves away from the sea, it is a broad arch "rising only 6 inches to 1 foot" (0.15-0.3 m).

Its parts are measured ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)):

- **Buttresses** between the channels are about 6 m wide and rise 0.6 m, with pink pavement on top and large globular coralline colonies on their sides.
- **Surge channels** are 1.5-3 m wide at the reef edge and open for 15-23 m. They then roof over, and water surges through holes and cracks in the roof with every wave, as far as 100 m from the edge.
- **Caverns** under the roof: one opening of 1.2 × 2.1 m widens below into a cavern 3 m wide and 2.4-3 m deep running to the reef edge. The roof is 0.3 m thick or less and spans up to 1.8 m, and the walls overhang at about 45° near the top.
- **Blowholes.** Long, narrow buttresses can end in "a crater-like algal mound" 0.76-0.9 m high round a blowhole, which at low tide spouts "violently and noisily with each incoming wave". Elsewhere the ridge is a "room-and-pillar" honeycomb of columns, or "hundreds of holes" from which water wells up quietly.
- **Behind the ridge,** algal rock masses up to 1.2 m high and solution-sculptured rocks up to 6 m across stand in the first 30 m. One fresh block of living pink alga, 3 × 3 × 1.2 m, lay a few feet from the hole it was torn from.

**Growth.** Three coralline algae divide the ridge. Globular Porolithon gardineri grows on the buttresses' seaward edges and in the upper channels. Pavement-forming P. onkodes covers the back slope. Columnar P. craspedium is commonest on leeward reefs and on reefs facing passes. The ridge is riddled with sea-urchin borings healed over by new algae ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). Its colour is recorded only as "pink", with "purple algae" behind (same source).

Corals here are the stout ones. Acropora cuneata grows on or just behind the ridge ([Wells via Marshall survey](https://rminucleardocs.icaad.ngo/en/entity/bqu6gixsctv?page=141)). At one reef edge "Pocillopora is the commonest coral", in colonies up to 0.3 m ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). At Tikehau a depression in front of the crest holds squat Pocillopora and Porites ([IRD Tikehau guide](https://horizon.documentation.ird.fr/exl-doc/pleins_textes/doc34-07/23077.pdf)). **Fire coral** (Millepora platyphylla) grows on shallow crests, in the surf zone and in spur-and-groove zones down to 10 m. On the Moorea crest it grows as vertical sheets on encrusting bases ([Wikipedia: M. platyphylla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millepora_platyphylla); [Moorea study, search summary](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/15014837/Population_structure_of_the_hydrocoral_Millepora_platyphylla.pdf)).

**Animals and reasons to visit.** The crest's food lives in the surf.

- **Snails.** Turbo setosus lives from the intertidal to 5 m on exposed seaward reef and is "one of the most frequently collected Turbinidae... mainly for food" ([SLB: Turbo setosus](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Turbo-setosus.html)). On Atafu, turban shells from the reef edges are, with giant clams, the only molluscs eaten ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)).
- **Urchins.** Slate-pencil urchins sit in depressions on the crest ([SLB: H. mamillatus](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Heterocentrotus-mamillatus.html)).
- **Sea cucumbers.** The surf redfish is "most abundant in the surf zone", at more than one per square metre, and is collected by hand while wading at low tide ([SLB: Actinopyga mauritiana](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Actinopyga-mauritiana.html)).
- **Lobster.** The pronghorn spiny lobster lives at 1-4 m, "often in surf zone and in surge channels" ([Kwajalein lobster](https://underwaterkwaj.com/uw-misc/lobster/Panulirus-penicillatus.htm)).
- **Fish.** Rabbitfish keep "typically in the surge zone at the reef edge" ([FB: Siganus argenteus](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Siganus-argenteus.html)), unicornfish in "strong surge" ([FB: Naso unicornis](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Naso-unicornis.html)), and drummers on "surf-swept outer reef flats" ([FB: Kyphosus cinerascens](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Kyphosus-cinerascens.html)). All three are drive-fishing targets. One Atafu seine drive by over 100 people took about 480-600 kg in an hour ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)).
- **Other uses.** Napuka men took sharks while standing on the reef ([ANU Press](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p337293/html/ch05.xhtml)). Tokelauans scooped a seabird, possibly the sooty tern, on the outer reef by day ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)). Yap had V-shaped stone fish traps on the crest ([Habele Institute](https://habeleinstitute.org/wiki/Zotero_reference:XQSZD2UI)).
- **Sights:** the blowholes at low tide and the break itself *(inference)*.

**Dangers.**

- **Surf.** No source covers people thrown onto an algal ridge. The nearest data are surfers on Hawaiian reef breaks: of 143 trauma-centre patients, 59% had fractures, 29% lacerations and 7% spinal-cord injury ([University of Hawaii thesis](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/items/45082fc4-16e3-4c82-8cce-728f59ab228c), search summary).
- **Fire coral** burns for hours. The rash lasts days and often returns after a week or two, and cuts with venom in them go necrotic and then badly infected ([DAN: Fire coral](https://dan.org/health-medicine/health-resources/diseases-conditions/fire-coral/)). Surge plus fire coral turns a fall into a cut that is also a sting *(inference)*.
- **Weather.** Diving the windward lobster zone takes "a very calm day" ([Kwajalein lobster](https://underwaterkwaj.com/uw-misc/lobster/Panulirus-penicillatus.htm)).
- **Getting back.** Re-entering through the break, and roofed caverns that a swimmer could be drawn into, are plausible hazards that no source describes *(inference)*.

**Along the shore.** The crest varies more along the shore than any other region. The two ridge types follow the curvature of the reef. The texture changes from honeycomb to long buttresses with blowholes to quiet seeping holes. Columnar algae replace pavement towards the pass ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). On the lee the ridge disappears altogether (see region 6).

### 4. The fore reef: spurs and white-sand grooves, and the most fish

**Ground and structures.** Bikini's swell-exposed sides have a shallow **terrace** "from a depth of 15 to 25 feet [4.6-7.6 m] at the reef edge to a depth of about 50 feet [15 m] at the outer edge". It is rather flat for several hundred yards, or slopes at 10-15°, below a slope of about 15° from low tide ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). The game's 55 m down to 10 m matches this. **Spurs and grooves** cross it:

- **Grooves** are 15-91 m long, 0.9-3 m wide and 1.8-7.6 m deep.
- **Spurs** are 7.6 m or more wide, with bluntly rounded seaward ends.
- **North of Enyu,** spurs are 46-91 m long and 3-12 m wide (7.6 m on average) under 0.9-2.4 m of water. Their seaward ends drop at 45° or more. The channels between them deepen from 2.4 m near the reef edge to 4.6-5.5 m at the outer margin, with walls 2.1-3 m high that are vertical or overhanging and have "coral growth abundant on the channel walls".
- **Groove floors** are "flat, rounded at the edges like the bottom of a bathtub": partly "barren, white, eroded rock", partly polished coral pebbles of 0.5-3 cm or boulders. Rock masses of 4.6 × 3 × 3 m have slid onto them.
- **Storm-collapsed notches** cut the south reef edges. They are 8-150 m wide, run 8-61 m into the reef, and are floored with blocks "veneered with living corals and algae". On the south-southeast they become a "comb" of narrow slots.

Source for all: [USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf). Spur width plus groove width implies a spur every 10-15 m on this atoll *(inference)*. Hawaiian spurs are larger: 0.7-1.6 m high, 71-104 m apart ([Storlazzi et al.](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20081109085456/http://walrus.wr.usgs.gov/staff/cstorlazzi/SAG_GSAbulletin.pdf)). How spurs form is disputed. One source calls them mainly built by coral growth ([Wikipedia: Spur and groove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spur_and_groove_formation)). Another calls the V-shaped Indo-Pacific kind, which runs on into surge channels, mainly cut by erosion ([JCU](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/62707/)).

**Growth.** On windward Tuamotu fore reefs, dense branching coral runs to 5-10 m: stout bushy Acropora, branching Pocillopora, finger Porites and small massive faviids ([Living Oceans](https://www.livingoceansfoundation.org/reefs-around-rangiroa-aratika-and-raraka/)). A shallow band of "stout, short branching corals (primarily Pocillopora, digitate and tabular Acropora)" is reported too ([Living Oceans field report, search summary](https://www.livingoceansfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/KSLOF-Field-Report_Tuamotu.pdf)). That puts the table, bushy Acropora and cauliflower models here and on the crest.

On the least-fished atolls, hard coral plus coralline algae covered 71% of the fore reef at 10-12 m at Kingman and 48% at Palmyra. Leafy seaweed covered under 1% at Kingman, and degraded Kiritimati was 68% turf and seaweed ([Sandin et al. 2008](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0001548)). Coral cover on Kingman's fore reef falls and becomes patchier as wave energy rises, and exposed fore reefs are the most diverse ([Williams et al. 2013](https://peerj.com/articles/81), search summary). On Bikini's spur tops coral covers about 20% ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)), so the coral should crowd the channel walls more than the spur tops. A healthy remote look is about 40-60% live coral, the rest pink crust and short turf, with clean white grooves *(inference)*. Fire coral grows on the spurs down to 10 m ([Wikipedia: M. platyphylla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millepora_platyphylla)).

**Animals and reasons to visit.** Adult reef fish are denser on atoll fore reefs than in lagoons ([atoll fish assemblages](https://www.alr-journal.org/10.1051/alr:2006021)). On unfished Palmyra, apex predators were 56% of fish biomass ([Sandin et al. 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2244711)).

- **Lobster by day.** At Kwajalein, pronghorn lobsters hide by day in "caves and ledges within the groove and spur system of the shallow windward reef" ([Kwajalein lobster](https://underwaterkwaj.com/uw-misc/lobster/Panulirus-penicillatus.htm)). This is the day-time half of the lobster loop.
- **Shellfish by diving.** The fluted clam T. squamosa is "abundant on outer reef slope" ([SLB: T. squamosa](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Tridacna-squamosa.html)). Turbo argyrostomus lives from the intertidal to 30 m ([SLB](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Turbo-argyrostomus.html)).
- **Fish under ledges.** Soldierfish live in caves and under ledges at 3-15 m ([FB: Myripristis berndti](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Myripristis-berndti.html)). Humphead wrasse rove the reefs by day and rest under ledges at night ([FB: Cheilinus undulatus](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Cheilinus-undulatus.html)).
- **Parrotfish.** Parrotfish, such as the steephead at 1-15 m ([FB: Chlorurus microrhinos](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Chlorurus-microrhinos.html)), were the main catch of Atafu's drives ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)).
- **Sharks.** Whitetip reef sharks rest in caves by day and hunt crevices at night, most commonly at 8-40 m ([Wikipedia: Whitetip reef shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitetip_reef_shark)).
- **Diving range.** Tuamotu pearl divers ordinarily dived to about 18-27 m for 60-90 seconds ([Kunz & Stevenson via karipearls](https://karipearls.com/divers.html)), so 10 m is comfortable range for a fit swimmer *(inference)*.

**Dangers.**

- **Fire coral** on the spur tops ([Wikipedia: M. platyphylla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millepora_platyphylla)).
- **Lionfish** shelter head-in in crevices by day, spines outward ([Wikipedia: Pterois antennata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pterois_antennata)). Scorpionfish are second only to stingrays among the causes of an estimated 40,000-50,000 marine-life injuries a year ([The Hospitalist](https://community.the-hospitalist.org/content/aquatic-antagonists-scorpionfish-envenomation)).
- **Crown-of-thorns starfish.** Juveniles hide under rubble on the fore-reef slope to 8 m. A puncture brings "immediate and often incapacitating" pain and swelling that can last a week ([Wikipedia: Crown-of-thorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown-of-thorns_starfish)).
- **Whitetips stealing catch.** Whitetips "readily attempt, and quite boldly, to steal catches from spear fishers" and answer a speargun shot within seconds ([Wikipedia: Whitetip reef shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitetip_reef_shark)).
- **Ears.** A pressure difference of 0.35 bar can rupture some eardrums and 0.75 bar most, roughly 3.5 m and 7.5 m of descent without equalising ([DAN Alert Diver](https://alertdiver.eu/en_US/articles/the-ears-have-it/?print=pdf); the conversion to depth is the researcher's).
- **Surge in the grooves** *(unsourced)*.

**Along the shore.** Spur-and-groove height and spacing shrink as wave energy rises ([TU Delft](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:c647278b-6774-4c09-893b-1638812e7c59)). On the most exposed Great Barrier Reef reefs, grooves are longer, more closely spaced and turned to face the swell, and spurs are unlikely to form where the upper slope is steeper than 5° ([Duce et al. 2016](https://www.deepreef.org/2016/05/15/spur-groove/)). So the south face gets low, close-set spurs aligned north-south. The east and west flanks get taller, wider-spaced spurs turned obliquely. The north gets none *(inference from the cited rules)*.

### 5. The drop-off: plates, crust and big predators where coral thins

**Ground and structures.** Below the terrace, Bikini's outer slopes average 25° down to 366 m. On the windward side the terrace's outer edge is put at about 15 m in 1954 and at 18-27 m in 1948; the two may describe parts of one feature. The leeward side has no terrace: a cliff runs "vertical from 100 ft [30 m] to more than 180 ft [55 m]" ([Emery 1948](https://thedirtydozenexpeditions.squarespace.com/s/EMERY-K-O-1948-SUBMARINE-GEOLOGY-OF-BIKINI-ATOLL-GSA-1948.pdf); [USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). Enewetak's windward slope averages about 60° between 90 and 365 m ([Colin et al. 1986, search summary](https://www.mesophotic.org/publications/8)). South Bikini's reef edges are "cavernous", parts near the surface overhang those below, and slumped sections up to 12 m long rest on the terrace ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)).

Real atolls have no 30 m sea floor; their slopes continue into the deep, so the game's floor is a convenience *(inference)*. It is best dressed as the sandy foot of a terrace:

- Halimeda debris dominates the slope sediment from 37 to 366 m ([Emery 1948](https://thedirtydozenexpeditions.squarespace.com/s/EMERY-K-O-1948-SUBMARINE-GEOLOGY-OF-BIKINI-ATOLL-GSA-1948.pdf)).
- Halimeda dominates sandy slopes from 1 m down to 30-50 m on Seychelles-area atolls ([search summary](https://repository.si.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/87105609-0401-4d89-b267-2294ed6305bf/content)).
- Garden eels colonise sandy slopes in steady current at 7-45 m, in dozens to hundreds, each facing the flow from its burrow, up to 40 per square metre in Hawaii ([Florida Aquarium](https://www.flaquarium.org/explore/habitats/shorelines/garden-eel/)).
- Wave-cut notches at about 24 and 35 m and old caves exist in the Maldives (Indian Ocean; [J. Coastal Research 2014, search summary](https://complete.bioone.org/journals/journal-of-coastal-research/volume-30/issue-1/JCOASTRES-D-12-00108.1/Submarine-Morphology-in-the-Maldives-and-Holocene-Sea-Level-Rise/10.2112/JCOASTRES-D-12-00108.1.full)).

**Growth.** At 10-20 m, larger boulder and plating corals. At 20-30 m, plating Porites and the overlapping "lettuce" sheets of Pachyseris ([Living Oceans](https://www.livingoceansfoundation.org/reefs-around-rangiroa-aratika-and-raraka/)). Off the Austral Islands, which are high islands, large Acropora cytherea tables, massive Pavona clavus and stout staghorn thickets grow at 20-25 m, and flattened Porites domes "over 5 m across and centuries old" lie below 25 m ([Living Oceans: Raivavae and Tubuai](https://www.livingoceansfoundation.org/lets-name-the-zones-the-zones-the-zones-of-the-reefs-of-raivavae-and-tubuai/)). That comparison is the strongest case for some table and staghorn models on the upper drop-off. The leeward Bikini cliff has under 10% coral, globular coralline algae down to 6-7.6 m, Halimeda, and pale patches on small ledges. Corals reach about 64 m ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). Black corals want hard rock in current, often beside crevices, and are mostly a below-50 m fauna ([Wikipedia: Antipatharia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipatharia)).

**Animals and reasons to visit.** The first 50 m of a drop-off shelters reef fish against the cliff ([Wikipedia: Coral reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reef)). The drop-off is the big-fish zone:

- red bass, "often near steep outer reef slopes" at 10-70 m ([FB: Lutjanus bohar](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Lutjanus-bohar.html));
- adult longface emperors "deep along coastal slopes and drop-offs" ([FB: L. olivaceus](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Lethrinus-olivaceus.html));
- dogtooth tuna round reefs, down to 250 m ([FB: Gymnosarda unicolor](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Gymnosarda-unicolor.html));
- slipper lobsters, usually at 20-50 m ([SLB: Scyllarides squammosus](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Scyllarides-squammosus.html));
- giant trevally, whose older fish keep to deeper seaward reefs and drop-offs ([Wikipedia: Giant trevally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_trevally)).

Just outside, Atafu trolled for skipjack and yellowfin, noosed wahoo, and set shark lines in 80-100 fathoms ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)). Off Enewetak, grey reef sharks form loose groups of 5-20 near drop-offs in the morning, growing through the day ([Wikipedia: Grey reef shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_reef_shark)). Two sights fit here:

- **A manta cleaning station** on a bommie at about 8-15 m (Maldives, operator sources; [Maldives Magazine](https://maldives-magazine.com/diving/diving-northern-highlights-guide.html)), on a current-swept corner *(inference)*.
- **A grouper spawning promontory.** Camouflage groupers spawn "in tide-influenced channels and on outer-slope promontories" ([SCRFA](https://www.scrfa.org/aggregations/aggregating-species/epinephelus-polyphekadion-camouflage-grouper/)).

The 30 m floor sits at the edge of a trained free diver's range. Pearl divers' measured dives were 80-107 seconds at 18-27 m, and longer claims are doubtful ([karipearls](https://karipearls.com/divers.html)).

**Dangers.**

- **Shallow-water blackout** governs here. DAN counted 565 breath-hold incidents in 2004-2012, 433 of them fatal. The blackout strikes near the surface on the way up and is linked to hyperventilating before the dive ([DAN/Alert Diver](https://alertdiver.eu/en_US/articles/dan-begins-reporting-on-incident-collection-of-breath-hold-diving/); [MJA](https://www.mja.com.au/node/14966)).
- **Grey reef sharks** give a "hunch" threat display that intensifies when a diver follows or corners them, and several attacks on spearfishers were probably aimed at the speared fish ([Wikipedia: Grey reef shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_reef_shark)).
- **Silvertips** rise from deeper water, "inquisitive and bold, especially in the presence of food" ([Wikipedia: Silvertip shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvertip_shark)).
- **Giant trevally.** One rammed a Hawaiian spearfisher hard enough to break three ribs ([Wikipedia: Giant trevally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_trevally)).
- **Ciguatera.** Above all, the drop-off's fish are the ones most likely to poison. Among Fiji's ciguatera cases, red bass caused 40%, barracuda 22% and groupers 12% ([Marine Toxins in the Island Pacific](https://repository.usp.ac.fj/id/eprint/5176/1/Marine_Toxins_in_the_Island_Pacific.pdf)).

**Along the shore.** The swell-facing south has a terrace with spurs that ends in a moderate slope. The lee has no terrace and a lumpy, algae-crusted wall. Storm notches with block-floored bays break the south edge. A promontory is the place for the spawning gathering, and the sand below a spur is the place for garden eels *(inference from the sources above)*.

## The lee, the pass and the inlet change the rules of the rim

### 6. The sheltered north: a ridge-less sill of tables, soft coral and zoanthids

**Ground and structures.** Leeward reefs "have no true ridge": the margin rises "only a few inches" above the flat ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). Leeward reef flats are often narrower, incomplete, cut by channels or sandy ([Springer chapter](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-94-007-5965-7_6)). On Colombian atolls the leeward reefs are absent or ill-defined, separated by wide gaps ([Living Oceans, attribution approximate](https://livingoceansfoundation.org/education/portal/course/reef-zonation)). This matches the game's "broken sill about 2 m deep with patches of reef".

Bikini's leeward flat is the best model for the sill. It is hard rock 1.2-1.5 m below low tide, with **tabular coral masses 1.8 m or more across** rising 0.6-0.9 m, and pools between them floored with coarse coral sand. No part of a 14 km stretch dries at low tide. Its northwest flat is "studded with tabular coral colonies several feet in diameter" in 0.3-0.6 m ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)).

A ridge-less reef edge north of Enyu gives a sourced cross-section, from the reef edge inward:

**Growth.** The lee should look different from the south. More than half of that soft-coral zone is "veneered with soft olive-green soft coral" in a net-like pattern. The zoanthid zone has 5-70% cover of silver-dollar-sized Palythoa with Pocillopora only 8-10 cm across ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). Tuamotu leeward reefs carry Porites, plating Montipora, small Favia boulders and Pavona columns, and Fakarava's leeward reef at 5 m had high cover of plating and submassive Porites ([Living Oceans](https://www.livingoceansfoundation.org/reefs-around-rangiroa-aratika-and-raraka/)). The coralline alga there is the columnar P. craspedium ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). Sea grapes grow tall in calm water ([SLB: Caulerpa racemosa](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Caulerpa-racemosa.html)). Lobed Porites, tables and staghorn belong here; stout Pocillopora and fire-coral blades largely do not *(inference)*.

**Animals and reasons to visit.** The lee is the fallback when the swell is up. On Napuka, most fishing in the December-March swell season was on the sheltered side ([ANU Press](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p337293/html/ch05.xhtml)). Honeycomb grouper and blacktail snapper favour "semi-protected seaward reefs" ([FB: E. merra](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Epinephelus-merra.html); [FB: Lutjanus fulvus](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Lutjanus-fulvus.html)). Grey reef sharks favour "leeward reef sides with clear water and rugged relief" ([Wikipedia: Grey reef shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_reef_shark)). On Palmyra, turf grazing was about five times higher on the sheltered terrace than on the exposed fore reef ([Palmyra foraging study](https://int-res.com/abstracts/meps/v501/p141-155)), which points to busy schools of herbivores *(inference)*.

The lee is not always calm. At Raroia, waves come from three directions, each dominant on one side of the atoll ([Aucan et al. 2021](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00705/81690/86749.pdf)):

- a northwest swell of up to 2 m from November to April;
- a southwest swell of up to 3 m from June to October;
- an easterly trade-wind sea of up to 3 m nearly all year.

A seasonal swell from the other hemisphere could therefore break on the game's lee now and then *(inference)*.

**Dangers.** Calm water lets people dive deeper and stay down longer, which brings the blackout and ear risks of the drop-off up onto the sill *(inference)*. Grey reef sharks, crown-of-thorns on the coral, and lionfish and morays in the holes all apply ([Wikipedia: Grey reef shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_reef_shark); [Wikipedia: Crown-of-thorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown-of-thorns_starfish)). Palythoa zoanthids are a well-known source of palytoxin, a hazard to anyone who handles or eats them *(unsourced here)*.

**Along the shore.** The sill should alternate between gaps and channels, table-coral patches, soft-coral carpets and zoanthid mats. Coral cover is higher but diversity lower than on the exposed side ([Williams et al. 2013](https://peerj.com/articles/81)).

### 7. The pass: the lagoon's drain, and the place where sharks and groupers gather

**Ground and structures.** On atolls with both, the deep pass is the outflow and the shallow channels across the reef flat (*hoa*) are the inflow. At Raroia, waves breaking on the rim "drive current into the lagoon, through the shallow hoa". The water raises the whole lagoon and "is evacuated back into the open ocean through a deep reef pass". The lagoon's tide there has half the ocean's range and lags it by 3-4 hours ([Aucan et al. 2021](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00705/81690/86749.pdf)).

The game's pass, 45 m wide and 9 m deep, is small. Ahe's single pass is 11 m deep and 200 m wide ([Dumas et al. 2012](https://data-ww3.ifremer.fr/BIB/Dumas_etal_MPB2012.pdf)), and Fakarava's south pass is about 90-200 m wide and about 30 m deep ([Undercurrent](https://undercurrent.org/UCnow/dive_magazine/2016/SharkPopulationFakarava201609.html)). The water will run through it as a tight jet *(inference)*.

Bikini's Enyu Channel, a 16 km wide opening far larger than this pass, has a "flat and composed of coarse white debris" floor with a low coral ridge rising 4.6-6 m from it. The ridge is about 46 m wide, its top "a jagged mass of living and dead coral, largely Acropora", with a steep 1.5 m wall at its base. Near passes, the reef flat becomes a rock flat "strewn with coral-head boulders" ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). General inlet theory says the throat stays scoured, the ebb builds a sand lobe seaward and the flood one landward ([Coastal Wiki](https://www.coastalwiki.org/wiki/Morphology_of_estuaries)). The pass therefore wants a bare white-rubble throat, scattered coral heads, and a sand lobe off its seaward mouth *(inference)*.

**Growth.** Near passes, "Millepora becomes more important than the true corals", and columnar coralline algae face the passes ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). At Tiputa Pass, Rangiroa, dive guides describe walls with "gorgonian fans, black coral bushes, and soft corals", hard coral on the edges, and coral heads on the floor used as cleaning stations ([Dive Planner Pro](https://diveplannerpro.com/spots/pf/rangiroa/french-polynesia-tiputa-pass-rangiroa); weak, operator source). A survey of French Polynesian lagoons found relatively high coral and mollusc abundance at passes ([Aquatic Living Resources](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/aquatic-living-resources/article/spatial-patterns-of-benthic-invertebrate-assemblages-within-atoll-lagoons-importance-of-habitat-heterogeneity-and-considerations-for-marine-protected-area-design-in-french-polynesia/D3BAA6C0C7EF3246ADC3D0AE8DEE6C0D)). Stinging hydroids that look like feathery plants grow on rock in current ([SLB: Macrorhynchia philippina](https://sealifebase.org/summary/44213)).

**Animals and reasons to visit.** The pass holds the atoll's biggest single food event.

- **The grouper aggregation.** At Fakarava's south pass, up to 17,000 camouflage groupers gather "exclusively during the full moons of June and July". They arrive about two weeks early and spawn at dawn on the outgoing current, and they draw an estimated 700 sharks ([Jublier et al. 2020](https://2025.maralliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Jublier-et-al.-E_poly_acoustics_AquatConserv_2020.pdf), search summary; [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/magazine/article/shark-alley-breeding-grouper-french-polynesia-fakarava-atoll)). A 45 m pass suggests a few dozen grey reef sharks, as at Rangiroa's groups of about 30, rather than a "wall" *(inference)*.
- **Other channel fish.** Roving coral grouper (Plectropomus laevis) use "channels and outer shelf reefs" and form perhaps one or two aggregations per reef ([FB: Plectropomus laevis](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Plectropomus-laevis.html)). Napoleon wrasse hold channel slopes ([FB: Cheilinus undulatus](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Cheilinus-undulatus.html)). Bonefish mass-spawn "at seaward mouths of channels" ([FB: Albula glossodonta](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Albula-glossodonta.html)).
- **Sea cucumbers.** The prickly redfish lives "near passes", weighs 2.5-7 kg and ranks among the highest-value sea cucumbers ([SLB: Thelenota ananas](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Thelenota-ananas.html)).
- **Dolphins** are said to surf Tiputa's standing wave ([Dive Planner Pro](https://diveplannerpro.com/spots/pf/rangiroa/french-polynesia-tiputa-pass-rangiroa); weak).
- **Traps and lines.** Napuka set coral traps in lagoon-to-sea channels ([ANU Press](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p337293/html/ch05.xhtml)). Aitutaki built stone-walled enclosures across channels to catch fish on the ebb ([Buck](https://nzetc.victoria.ac.nz/tm/scholarly/tei-BucMate-t1-body-d8-d5.html)). Atafu fished hook and line "mainly around reef channels" ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)).

**Dangers.** The current is the deadly one.

- **Speed.** In the Cook Islands, currents in reef passages can exceed 15 km/h (about 4.2 m/s), and locals say they pull people under as well as out. The advice is to swim across the current, not against it ([Cook Islands News](https://cookislandsnews.com/?p=252780)). Tiputa's quoted 3-8 knots are not instrumented ([Dive Planner Pro](https://diveplannerpro.com/spots/pf/rangiroa/french-polynesia-tiputa-pass-rangiroa)).
- **When it is worst.** Because the south swell pumps water over the south crest and it leaves here, the outflow should be strongest on the ebb in big swell, and big swell alone should keep it running out even on the flood *(inference from Raroia)*.
- **Swept out to sea.** A swimmer carried out faces exhaustion and slow cooling: hypothermia happens even in 29-33 °C water over hours ([DAN: Hypothermia](https://alertdiver.eu/en_US/articles/hypothermia/)). Oceanic whitetips live in the open ocean beyond ([Wikipedia: Oceanic whitetip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_whitetip_shark)).
- **Sharks.** Grey reef sharks rest in the pass by day and hunt at night in loose packs ([CNRS News, search summary](https://news.cnrs.fr/node/1308)). Tiger sharks move into channels after prey and come inshore at night ([Wikipedia: Tiger shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_shark)).
- **Weather.** On Atafu, November-March westerlies make the passes on the village side "problematical" ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)).

**Along the shore.** The pass spreads its influence along the rim on either side: boulder-strewn rock flats, more fire coral than coral, and columnar pink algae ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). The flanks of the pass therefore look unlike the reef a hundred metres away.

### 8. The inlet and its sand fan: a channel for weirs and bonefish runs

**Ground and structures.** Hoa are shallow channels across the reef flat, from a few centimetres to about 2 m deep. Tuamotu atolls have 10-12, and up to 50 ([IRD](https://horizon.documentation.ird.fr/exl-doc/pleins_textes/divers20-05/010027643.pdf)). At Anaa, which has no deep pass, the back-reef channels between islets shrink to 10-20 cm at low water ([Filous et al. 2020](https://www.fecpl.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Filous-et-al.-2020-bonefish-spawning-telemity-EBF.pdf)).

Which way the inlet runs is open. Raroia's hoa on the wave-exposed rim always ran towards the lagoon on daily average, at up to 0.5-0.6 m/s. On atolls without a pass, hoa on the side away from the waves drain outward ([Aucan et al. 2021](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00705/81690/86749.pdf)). The game's inlet is on the lee and the atoll has a pass, so the inlet should swing with the tide and probably run outward when a big south swell holds the lagoon up *(inference)*. A sand fan on its lagoon side reads as a flood delta, which says the flood carries more sand in than the ebb takes out. A fan on the sea side would be an ebb delta ([Coastal Wiki](https://www.coastalwiki.org/wiki/Morphology_of_estuaries); applying it to this inlet is *(inference)*). No atoll-specific data on fan size or texture were found.

**Growth and animals.** The fan is sand country: rays and blacktips on the bottom, goatfish and mullet. Ghost-shrimp mounds would suit finer sand *(inference; see the lagoon)*.

**Reasons to visit.** The inlet is a building project that pays on a lunar calendar.

- **Bonefish runs.** At Anaa, bonefish gather in their thousands at 85-92% moon illumination after full moon. On the 4th or 5th day after full moon they cross the shallow channels and wait "for nightfall to exit the atoll", from March to September, peaking in April-May. Permanent rock weirs across the channels caught up to 1,000 fish a day; bonefish were up to 49% of the catch, and at least 62 species were taken in all. Blacktip, whitetip and grey reef sharks chase the gathered fish ([Filous et al. 2020](https://www.fecpl.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Filous-et-al.-2020-bonefish-spawning-telemity-EBF.pdf)).
- **Weirs.** Atafu built stone weirs about 18 m long, mouth facing land and lagoon, for schools of trevally and garfish. It also set nets on the reef flat for fish "moving between the reef and lagoon during low tide" ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)).
- **Moon timing on Atafu.** Rabbitfish cross on the 27th-29th nights of the lunar month. Convict surgeonfish come on the 1st-3rd and 22nd-25th. Small trevally and emperors cross on the 15th-16th and 27th-28th nights, from May to November. Fishers count nights 1-10 as "ocean nights" and 15-30 as "nights of the lagoon" (same source).
- **Mullet.** Fringelip mullet form large schools "at the beginning of the ebb tide (in June)" and spawn after dark ([FB: C. crenilabis](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Crenimugil-crenilabis.html)).

**Dangers.** Rays buried in the sand ([Wikipedia: Stingray injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stingray_injury)). Blacktips feeding on the runs ([Filous et al. 2020](https://www.fecpl.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Filous-et-al.-2020-bonefish-spawning-telemity-EBF.pdf)). The flow in the channel. Stonefish in rubble *(inference)*. Needlefish at night: they are "greatly excited by artificial light at night" and leap at up to 60 km/h; a 10-year-old boy fishing at night on Kaua'i was killed when one pierced his eye and brain ([Wikipedia: Needlefish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Needlefish)).

## The lagoon: Porites knolls on foram sand around a silt-floored hole

At 250 m long, the game's lagoon is tiny beside Bikini's saucer, which falls to a 62 m basin ([Emery 1948](https://thedirtydozenexpeditions.squarespace.com/s/EMERY-K-O-1948-SUBMARINE-GEOLOGY-OF-BIKINI-ATOLL-GSA-1948.pdf)). The better models are small, poorly flushed Tuamotu lagoons. Takapoto's lagoon has few channels, slow renewal and high salinity. Its fauna is sparse, about 14 coral genera and mostly Porites, with the best patches in the south ([Takapoto](https://scilit.com/publications/e8a17c28c6cb587786997aa3b1f1cad8)). Tatakoto's semi-enclosed lagoon swings 3-4 °C a day, to almost 35 °C, yet holds branching Acropora with giant clams and massive Porites rising to the surface ([CNRS](https://images.cnrs.fr/en/node/30775)). Lagoon water should be warmer, coral less diverse, and the best coral nearest where ocean water enters *(inference)*.

### The sand shelf (1.6 m)

**Ground.** Near shore the lagoon floor is coarse foram sand. Grain size fines towards the lagoon centre, and fine carbonate sand and silt cover the deeper floor ([Emery 1948](https://thedirtydozenexpeditions.squarespace.com/s/EMERY-K-O-1948-SUBMARINE-GEOLOGY-OF-BIKINI-ATOLL-GSA-1948.pdf); [Midway and Kure](https://core.ac.uk/works/2337258)). Patch reefs near Bikini's lagoon shore are zoned in rings, not bands. An inner zone of mostly sand with sparse corals in 0.6-0.9 m abuts eroded beachrock. Outside it is a middle zone with blue-coral heads up to 1.8 m rising 0.6 m over sand bound by living Halimeda. On the outer edge a lobed margin carries coral and soft-coral heads 1.2-6 m across, with large areas of yellow-green fire coral. Many colonies are dead and filmed with brown algae ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). This ringed edge is how the shelf should meet the basin *(inference)*. Seagrass (Thalassia, Cymodocea, Halodule) occurs patchily on Micronesian atolls such as Majuro and Kayangel ([Micronesica 1977](https://www.micronesica.org/sites/default/files/distribution_of_seagrasses_in_micronesia-micronesica_vol._13_no._2_dec._1977o.pdf)).

**Animals and reasons to visit.**

- **Fish.** Bonefish feed in the lagoon and gather over sand before spawning ([Filous et al. 2020](https://www.fecpl.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Filous-et-al.-2020-bonefish-spawning-telemity-EBF.pdf)). The bigeye emperor Monotaxis feeds at night over "sand and rubble areas near coral reefs" ([FB: Monotaxis grandoculis](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Monotaxis-grandoculis.html)).
- **Clams and conchs.** The bear-paw clam "usually sits loose on sandy or rocky bottoms" and can simply be lifted ([Kwajalein clams](https://www.underwaterkwaj.com/shell/tridacna/tridacnidae.htm)). Old giant spider conchs are crusted with coralline algae, which camouflages them ([SLB: Lambis truncata](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Lambis-truncata.html)).
- **Sea grapes.** Caulerpa lentillifera grows on "shallow, sandy to muddy lagoon and reef flats which are not exposed during low tides" ([SLB: C. lentillifera](https://sealifebase.org/summary/Caulerpa-lentillifera), search summary). Fiji harvests about 110 t of sea grapes a year from reef flats at low tide ([J. Applied Phycology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10811-014-0254-1), search summary).
- **Clam beds.** Atafu levers giant clams "from below the water with a knife-like instrument called a *nao*" in the shallow lagoon. Taking small ones is forbidden ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)). At Helen Reef, large T. gigas and T. derasa lie along lagoon margins in 2-3 m ([NMFS Marine Fisheries Review](https://fisherybulletin.nmfs.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/pdf-content/MFR/mfr367/mfr3675.pdf)). That is the sourced home for a field of the game's largest clams, where the species' range allows it.
- **Place names.** Atafu's own names for lagoon zones would make authentic labels for the game's places: *matafaga* for the shallow tidal margin, *aloalo* for "much deeper but visible bottom", *akau* for coral formations in the interior (same source).

### The basin (4 m) and its patch reefs

**Structures.** Small knolls are 3-15 m across and 0.9-6 m high. "Smaller knolls that rise from shallower bases are usually 10 to 30 feet [3-9 m] in diameter and grow 10 or 20 feet [3-6 m] to the surface with near-vertical and sometimes overhanging sides", from "a flat sand or gravel bottom with little or no apron of debris" ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). Low mounds a few feet high cover about 20% of a protected Rongelap bay floor and show as dark patches from the air (same source). Knoll tops are flattened and cemented by a coralline crust, with 50-75% or more live coral: massive Porites "several feet in diameter", Acropora brackets up to 3 m long, and "large thickets of staghorn Acropora" in hollows. Living Halimeda fills the pockets, and its white segments make much of the sand. The sides are "white... much lighter than the top", debris-covered, with little coral; other knolls are mostly loose fragments forming a talus slope of about 45° (same source). Bare sand **halos** metres to tens of metres wide ring patch reefs. They scale with reef size and herbivore numbers and are caused mainly by grazers that stay close to cover ([Frontiers 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2019.00102/full)). On fine lagoon sand, ghost-shrimp burrow mounds half a metre to a metre across can be large and dense ([Underwater Kwajalein](https://underwaterkwaj.com/uw-misc/shrimp/Callianassidae-sp1.htm)). Lagoon patch reefs at Kingman are near-exclusively slow-growing Porites ([Williams et al. 2013](https://peerj.com/articles/81)).

The sources disagree on fire coral in lagoons. Bikini's lagoon patch reefs have "large areas" of Millepora on their outer margins ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)), while Moorea's lowest Millepora densities are in lagoons ([Moorea study, search summary](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/15014837/Population_structure_of_the_hydrocoral_Millepora_platyphylla.pdf)). Fringe patches near the pass and leave the rest bare *(inference)*.

**Animals and reasons to visit.**

- **Lagoon fish.** Atafu's lagoon fish are squirrelfish, butterflyfish, damselfish, goatfish, rabbitfish, and small groupers, emperors, triggerfish and wrasses. Atafu takes them on night lines, after tipping a sack of coral gravel into the water to draw fish (*tuki akau*), or by swimming with goggles and an octopus-baited line ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)).
- **Clams on knoll tops.** A Bikini knoll top held numerous giant clams up to 20 cm, some of 0.3-0.6 m and one of 0.9 m ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)).
- **Pearl oysters** attach by threads to rock and coral debris in "lagoon reef areas", usually at 0-20 m ([SLB: Pinctada margaritifera](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Pinctada-margaritifera.html)). Their shell made Atafu's lure shanks. Atafu's oysters were wiped out by the 1950s after diving goggles arrived ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)), so a fresh, unworked oyster bed is a rare find *(inference)*.
- **Garfish** are taken by torch and scoop net inside the lagoon at night (same source).

### The 13 m hole

No source described an Indo-Pacific lagoon hole, so most of this is inference. The Great Blue Hole in Belize is a drowned limestone sinkhole 124 m deep that formed as a cave at low glacial sea level ([Wikipedia: Great Blue Hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Blue_Hole)). That explains how a hole can exist, not what a 13 m one looks like.

Since the finest silt collects in the deepest parts of lagoons ([Midway and Kure](https://core.ac.uk/works/2337258)), the hole should have steep knoll-like walls, dimming blue light and a pale silt floor *(inference)*. Its fauna is assembled from species depth ranges, not observation:

- big loose fluted clams;
- true giant clams, which rest loose and upright to 30 m, where the range allows ([Kwajalein clams](https://www.underwaterkwaj.com/shell/tridacna/tridacnidae.htm));
- prickly redfish, found in lagoons to 30 m ([SLB: Thelenota ananas](https://www.sealifebase.se/summary/Thelenota-ananas.html));
- red bass, which also enter sheltered lagoons ([FB: L. bohar](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Lutjanus-bohar.html));
- Napoleon wrasse on lagoon reefs ([FB: C. undulatus](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Cheilinus-undulatus.html)).

It is a diving-only reward.

Bad water is unlikely. Documented sulphide layers sit at about 15 m in a sealed marine lake in Palau and at 40-100 m in atoll blue holes, where circulation is poor ([Wikipedia: Jellyfish Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jellyfish_Lake); [Wikipedia: Blue hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_hole)). A hole flushed by a pass should be clean, and a toxic layer would need an enclosed pond *(inference)*. The real dangers are these:

- **Ears.** 13 m is well past the depth at which unequalised eardrums rupture ([DAN Alert Diver](https://alertdiver.eu/en_US/articles/the-ears-have-it/?print=pdf)).
- **Blackout** on the ascent ([MJA](https://www.mja.com.au/node/14966)).
- **Silt-out.** In caves, most deaths follow getting lost when fins stir up silt ([Potts et al. 2016](https://www.dhmjournal.com/images/IndividArticles/46Sept/Potts_dhm.46.3.150-154.pdf)). Under a ledge in the hole, a free diver would have seconds *(inference)*.

### The sandbank islet

No source covers lagoon sand cays directly. The nearest is uninhabited Jemo in the Marshalls, 16 ha, which "once held huge rookeries of seabirds and turtles" and is a destination for hunting and harvest trips ([Island Conservation](https://www.islandconservation.org/restoring-rmi-an-adventure-of-hope/), search summary). Tokelauans caught terns and noddies with nets and nooses ([Ono & Addison 2009](https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/static/Journals/080609/080609-26.pdf)). Sooty-tern eggs were a rough-season food in the Seychelles ([Inch et al. 2024](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10192027/1/2024%20Inch%20et%20al%20Ibis.pdf), search summary). The islet is the sourced home of an egg-and-bird trip.

It also carries a sourced seasonal danger. At French Frigate Shoals, tiger sharks patrol the shallow lagoon off a seabird islet in July, when young albatrosses crash into the water learning to fly, and leave once the birds disperse ([University of Hawaii](https://www.hawaii.edu/news/2025/12/17/turf-wars-sharks-seabirds/)). Tiger sharks are second only to white sharks in recorded fatal attacks ([Wikipedia: Tiger shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_shark)).

### What threatens the lagoon swimmer

- **Rays.** The pink whipray, the "fai" of French Polynesia, forms sizable night feeding groups in shallow water at Rangiroa ([Wikipedia: Pink whipray](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Himantura_fai), search summary).
- **Titan triggerfish** females guard nests in flat sand inside a cone-shaped territory that widens upward. The escape is sideways, not up ([Wikipedia: Titan triggerfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_triggerfish)).
- **Patch-reef animals:** urchins, morays, lionfish and crown-of-thorns, as in the gardens.
- **Ciguatera** is invisible and fixed in place. At Moorea the risk map stayed stable from the late 1970s to 2016, with the north shore still worst ([HAL Moorea](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01391648), search summary). The algae that make the toxin live on algae and coral rubble and flare after reef damage ([Marine Toxins in the Island Pacific](https://repository.usp.ac.fj/id/eprint/5176/1/Marine_Toxins_in_the_Island_Pacific.pdf)).
- **Sharks.** At Palmyra, adult blacktips keep out of the deeper central lagoon, avoiding patrolling tiger sharks ([Wikipedia: Blacktip reef shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacktip_reef_shark)). Their absence over the basin is a warning sign a careful player could learn *(inference)*.

## Same distance out, different reef: the sourced causes

**Exposure is the master variable.** Lateral zoning follows exposure to waves, which divides an atoll into windward and leeward reefs ([Atoll Research Bulletin, search summary](https://dn720400.ca.archive.org/0/items/atollresearchbu435449smit/atollresearchbu435449smit_hocr.html)). Fish and bottom communities on windward fore reef, leeward fore reef and lagoon patch reef are each distinct, in a Caribbean atoll used here only as an analogue ([Glover's Reef](https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/maec.12129)). A single exposure value round the rim can drive the transition *(inference)*:

- **The south** gets the full ridge, the surge channels and the ramparts.
- **The east**, facing the wind, gets a lesser version.
- **The west and north** grade to the ridge-less sill.

The cited rules above say what the value controls: spur spacing, ridge height, Pocillopora and fire coral against Porites and plates, and coralline algae changing from globular to pavement to columnar.

**Reef curvature makes the biggest change at one distance out.** Where the reef bulges seaward, the ridge stands 0.6-1.1 m with surge channels, and the flat behind is nearly bare. Where it curves away, the ridge is a 0.15-0.3 m arch with a rich coral flat behind ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf)). A coastline with gentle headlands and bays therefore produces alternating barren and lush gardens at the same 80 m, without any new rule *(inference)*.

**Storm history adds a patchwork on top.** Storm blocks crowd "certain reaches only". Notches up to 150 m wide cut the south edge. Cyclone damage is patchy and depends on coral make-up and bottom waves ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf); [Zinke et al.](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/90098/1/Zinke_et_al_revision_030217.pdf)). Windward sides usually suffered most from a cyclone and recovered little in 5-6 years ([JCU, Whitsundays](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/91209/1/Srinivasan%20et%20al.%202026%20%20Long-term%20cyclone%20effects%20on%20reefs%20and%20reef%20fish%20in%20the%20Whitsunday%20Islands.pdf)). French Polynesian reefs hit by cyclone, bleaching and crown-of-thorns returned to their former communities in 5-10 years ([Sci Rep](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41598-018-38228-5)). Chagos live coral fell from 42% to 5-15% after the 2015-16 bleaching ([KSLOF Chagos report](https://www.livingoceansfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Chagos-Archipelago-Final-Report.pdf)). Each stretch can carry a "disturbance age": fresh rubble and turf, a recovering mix, or an old thicket *(inference)*. Ciguatera rises with exactly that rubble, so the most broken reef should also hold the most dangerous fish *(inference from the toxin sources)*.

**The pass and the inlet pull the reef around them out of pattern.** Near them come boulder-strewn flats, fire coral and columnar algae. The pass holds higher mollusc and coral abundance, and fish movements are concentrated there ([USGS PP 260-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0260a/report.pdf); [Aquatic Living Resources](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/aquatic-living-resources/article/spatial-patterns-of-benthic-invertebrate-assemblages-within-atoll-lagoons-importance-of-habitat-heterogeneity-and-considerations-for-marine-protected-area-design-in-french-polynesia/D3BAA6C0C7EF3246ADC3D0AE8DEE6C0D)).

**Small biological patches reward a close look:**

- octopus middens at den mouths ([Wikipedia: Octopus cyanea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octopus_cyanea));
- white feeding scars that betray a hidden crown-of-thorns ([Wikipedia: Crown-of-thorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown-of-thorns_starfish));
- halos whose width tells how many grazers a patch reef holds ([Frontiers 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2019.00102/full));
- clam beds reserved for feasts ([ANU Press](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/p337293/html/ch05.xhtml)).

**Rare sights should be placed once each and tied to a cue in the geography** *(inference)*:

- the grouper gathering at the pass or a promontory;
- a manta cleaning bommie at 8-15 m on a current-swept corner;
- the big-clam field on a sheltered lagoon margin at 2-3 m;
- garden eels on the sand below a spur;
- turtle ledges near any seagrass ([Marine Biology 2019](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00227-019-3584-3.pdf));
- the one giant Porites.

Giant Porites heads are high-island records: 22.4 m across at Ta'u in American Samoa ([NOAA Sanctuaries](https://sanctuaries.noaa.gov/vr/american-samoa/big-momma/)). One of 8-10 m would already be a landmark *(inference)*.

## Danger follows ground, current and hour more than distance from shore

Most injuries are common, local and survivable. Ciguatera dominates by number of cases: 545-585 cases per 100,000 a year in French Polynesia, 324-462 in Kiribati and 653-1,338 in Tokelau in 1973-1983, with perhaps only a fifth of cases reported ([Marine Toxins in the Island Pacific](https://repository.usp.ac.fj/id/eprint/5176/1/Marine_Toxins_in_the_Island_Pacific.pdf)). Sharks are rare killers: ISAF counts 8 confirmed unprovoked attacks each in the Marshall Islands, Kiribati and French Polynesia since 1848 ([ISAF Oceania](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/shark-attacks/maps/oceania)). The table below ranks dangers by region. The ranking itself is assembled from habitat data; no source ranks them *(inference)*. Each listed danger is cited in its region above.

| Region | Common, mostly minor | Rare and severe | Worst when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ocean beach and shallows | Stingray stabs, blacktip nips to feet, sunburn and heat | Stonefish; a ray barb to the trunk after a fall | Low tide for stonefish; rising tide and night for rays and blacktips; midday |
| Gardens | Coral cuts (Vibrio), long-spined urchins, fire coral on pool rims | Stonefish, geographer cone, a moray in an octopus hole, eating a xanthid crab | Low spring tides; dusk and night, when urchins spread out |
| Crest | Surf lacerations, fire coral, urchins | Fractures and head injury in the break; being drawn into a cavern *(inference)* | Big swell; low tide |
| Fore reef | Fire coral, scorpionfish and lionfish, ear squeeze | Whitetip or grey reef bite in a scramble for speared fish; crown-of-thorns | Just after a spear shot; calm days that tempt long dives |
| Drop-off | Ear and sinus squeeze | Shallow-water blackout; grey reef and silvertip sharks; ciguatera from big fish | Long breath-holds after hyperventilating |
| North lee | As gardens and fore reef | Blackout, because calm water invites deep dives | Calm days |
| Pass | Fire coral and hydroids on the walls | Swept to sea (exhaustion, slow hypothermia, oceanic whitetips); sharks at night | Ebb on a big swell; June-July spawning nights |
| Inlet and fan | Rays, blacktips | Needlefish impalement | Night torch fishing; fish runs |
| Lagoon | Rays, urchins, morays, triggerfish nests | Ciguatera hot spots; tiger sharks; blackout or silt-out in the hole | Night; bird-fledging season; near broken reef |

The field treatments group neatly, which suits a survival game.

**Hot water for the heat-labile venoms.** Stonefish, scorpionfish, lionfish and stingray venoms, and probably urchin toxins, break down in hot water. The treatment is immersion at 42-45 °C for 30-90 minutes, with the rescuer testing the water, because numbed skin scalds ([The Hospitalist](https://community.the-hospitalist.org/content/aquatic-antagonists-scorpionfish-envenomation); [AAFP 2004](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2004/0215/p885.html); [Healthdirect](https://healthdirect.gov.au/sea-creature-stings)). For urchins, DAN recommends it but one review finds no human evidence ([DAN: Sea urchins](https://dan.org/health-medicine/health-resources/diseases-conditions/sea-urchins/); [AAFP 2004](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2004/0215/p885.html)). Scorpionfish spines stay venomous up to 48 hours after the fish dies ([StatPearls, search summary](https://www.statpearls.com/point-of-care/24293)), so cleaning the catch is a hazard in itself.

**Rescue breathing for the paralysing poisons.** Cone snail, blue-ringed octopus and pufferfish poisons all paralyse breathing, and blue-ring and pufferfish victims can stay fully aware while unable to breathe. None has an antidote; survival means keeping the victim breathing until it passes. Pufferfish survivors of the first 24 hours usually recover fully ([PubMed 27285461](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27285461/); [DAN: Blue-ringed octopus](https://dan.org/health-medicine/health-resources/diseases-conditions/blue-ringed-octopus/); [StatPearls: Tetrodotoxin, search summary](https://www.statpearls.com/point-of-care/30383)).

**Fire coral relapses.** Vinegar stabilises stinging cells that have not yet fired. The rash often returns after a week or two ([DAN: Fire coral](https://dan.org/health-medicine/health-resources/diseases-conditions/fire-coral/)).

**Ciguatera is slow.** It begins 30 minutes to 48 hours after the meal. Tingling and hot-cold reversal last weeks to months, and fewer than 1% die. Fish, alcohol and nuts bring it back for months, and it concentrates in the liver and guts ([StatPearls: Ciguatera, mirror](https://git.lsit.ucsb.edu/publicdata/nih-gov/raw/commit/a3492137cada35c1427e5783c176e4344e6cbb56/www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482511/index.html%3Freport=printable)). The traditional test was to feed the viscera to a cat ([Marine Toxins in the Island Pacific](https://repository.usp.ac.fj/id/eprint/5176/1/Marine_Toxins_in_the_Island_Pacific.pdf)).

**Turtle meat can kill without warning.** A healthy-looking hawksbill from a Micronesian atoll lagoon sickened 101 of 120 who ate it and killed six, four of them children ([Pavlin et al. 2015](https://ojs.wpro.who.int/ojs/index.php/wpsar/article/download/283/437?inline=1)).

## Range limits force a choice of longitude

The measured structures come from the Marshall Islands, but much of the food and pass life comes from the Tuamotus, and the two differ. The game should choose a setting. The right-hand column of the table is the implication for this atoll *(inference)*. A Marshalls-to-Kiribati atoll keeps the true giant clam, the bear-paw clam, patchy seagrass and blue coral. A Tuamotu atoll loses them and gains the best-documented pass gatherings *(inference)*.

Bull sharks were not checked and are probably absent from remote atolls *(unsourced)*.

## Conclusion

The research turns the game's depth intuition upside down. On an atoll, coral and clams are richest where light and moving water meet: the storm-side gardens with 50-75% coral, the crest edge where Pocillopora is the commonest coral, the knoll tops with 75% cover. The drop-off is where coral thins to plates, crust and Halimeda. The game's geometry is already right to within the scatter of real reef flats, so the work ahead is placement and dressing rather than reshaping. Two cheap inputs, an exposure value round the rim and a flag for headland or bay, would by themselves produce most of the documented variety at a given distance out. A patchwork of storm history laid over them would also secretly carry the ciguatera map.

The deeper finding is that time matters more than place. Almost every sourced resource and many dangers are gated by tide, moon or season:

- lobsters on the flat on a rising moonlit tide;
- bonefish through the inlet on the 4th-5th night after full moon;
- groupers in the pass at the June-July full moons;
- flying fish on dark nights from July to October;
- clams as the swell-season staple;
- stonefish exposed at low springs.

A still sea with no tide or moon cannot express most of what makes each region worth a visit, so a tide and lunar clock would unlock more of this research than any new model *(inference)*. The largest gaps that remain:

- nothing describes a small Indo-Pacific lagoon hole;
- no food is specific to the lee;
- no source gives sizes of caves, sand chutes or staghorn thickets;
- no ethnography says how islanders themselves ranked the dangers.

Johannes's *Words of the Lagoon* and the Pacific Community (SPC) field guides, which were unreachable here, are the obvious next sources.
